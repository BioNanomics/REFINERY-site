High school robots in Halloween costumes compete at Wayne High School Oct. 31 as Monster Match pairs a FIRST Robotics Competition with a college and career fair

Co-hosted by The REFINERY, Purdue FIRST Programs Fort Wayne, Region 8 Education Service Center and NEI TechFest. Free and open to the public; matches 8:15 a.m. to 6 p.m., college and career fair 10 a.m. to 2 p.m.

FORT WAYNE, Ind. – On Saturday, Oct. 31, high school robotics teams bring the robots they designed and built this year to Wayne High School for Monster Match, a Halloween-themed FIRST Robotics Competition offseason event. Seventeen teams are registered and the field stays open until it reaches 32.

The day combines a robotics competition, a college and career fair and a costume contest with categories for individuals, teams and robots. To be eligible in the robot category, a robot has to play its matches in costume. Admission is free. Matches run from 8:15 a.m. to 6 p.m.

Every January, FIRST Robotics Competition releases a new game to more than 3,700 high school teams worldwide at the same moment, and teams have about two months to design and build a machine to play it before competitions begin in March. This year's game, REBUILT, asked robots to collect foam balls, shoot them into a goal on a 54-foot field and climb a tower in the final seconds of each match.

The robots can weigh up to 115 pounds. Students do the work: they design in CAD, machine the metal parts, wire the electronics, write the code and drive in matches where alliances of three robots play against three.

Teams also run like small companies, raising their own money, recruiting sponsors and managing their own budgets. Participants are eligible for more than $80 million in college scholarships.

Offseason events like Monster Match give teams one more chance to compete with this year's robots and a chance to train newer members before the next game is released on Jan. 9. Because it falls on Halloween, Monster Match adds costume contests, trick-or-treating and a robot photo booth.

In the same building, colleges, employers and trade and technical schools host a college and career fair from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., putting students in front of the regional employers who need the skills they use to build a robot. Families can trick-or-treat through the exhibitor booths while the fair is open and watch matches throughout the day.

Monster Match is co-hosted by The REFINERY; Purdue FIRST Programs Fort Wayne, a student organization at Purdue University Fort Wayne; Region 8 Education Service Center, the cooperative of public, private and parochial schools serving 15 Northeast Indiana counties; and NEI TechFest, the Northeast Indiana Tech Coalition's annual event connecting high school students with the region's technology employers. Sponsors include Daniels Associates, Inc.; Resultant; the City of Fort Wayne; Indiana Tech; and Huntington University.

About The REFINERY

The REFINERY is Northeast Indiana's first robotics-only facility, a nonprofit makerspace at Electric Works operated by BioNanomics, a nonprofit. It gives FIRST Robotics Competition and FIRST Tech Challenge teams across a 12-county region the shop space, fabrication equipment, training and mentorship that many of them could not afford on their own. refineryrobotics.org

Story ideas

Robots in costume

The costume contest has a robot category, and a robot has to play its matches in costume to qualify. Students and whole teams dress up as well.

Keeping a robot alive for ten months

These robots were built in January and February and have been running ever since: two, three or four competitions for most teams, plus outreach events and public displays through the summer. Getting one through a full day of matches at the end of October means repairing, replacing and maintaining a machine that was never meant to last this long. Teams are doing that work right now.

Two brand-new teams built for this event

Team 1501 T.H.R.U.S.T. of Huntington County has created a second team to compete at Monster Match. At Homestead High School, the rookie members of 4982 Olympus Robotics built their own robot this fall and will compete with it on their own, without the veterans, for the first time.

A day in the pits

Students drive every match. Between matches the robot goes back to the pit, where students diagnose, repair and rebuild under the clock before the next match is called. A reporter can spend the day with one team and follow one robot from its first match to its last.

The REFINERY's first offseason event

Monster Match is the first offseason event The REFINERY has hosted. The REFINERY is a nonprofit shop at Electric Works that supports 15 FIRST teams and about 200 students across 12 counties.

What comes next

The 2027 game, BIOCORE, is released Jan. 9, 2027. The FIRST Indiana Robotics district event in Fort Wayne is April 1 to 3, 2027 at Homestead High School.

About FIRST

FIRST (For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology) is a nonprofit that runs team-based youth robotics programs from kindergarten through 12th grade. FIRST Robotics Competition, for grades 9 to 12, fielded 3,724 teams in 2026. Indiana competes as a district, FIRST Indiana Robotics, with 69 FRC teams. Participants are eligible for more than $80 million in college scholarships from about 200 providers.

A match is played by two alliances of three robots each, red against blue, on a 27 by 54 ft field. The first 20 seconds are autonomous; student drivers then take over for 2 minutes 20 seconds. Robots are built from a kit of parts plus custom-machined and 3D-printed components and can weigh up to 115 pounds.

REBUILT, the 2026 game

Robots collect foam balls called Fuel and shoot them into a central goal called the Hub. In the final seconds, robots climb a multi-level Tower for bonus points. The robots at Monster Match are the machines teams built for REBUILT in January and February 2026; after the official season ends in April, teams keep their robots and compete at volunteer-run offseason events like this one. Monster Match is played on an AndyMark field.
