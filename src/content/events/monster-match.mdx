Spend Halloween with the robots. The Monster Match brings high school robotics teams from across the region to Wayne High School for a day of robot competition, and the whole community is invited to come watch, trick or treat, and cheer them on. Admission is free, and no registration is needed to attend.

## Watch the robots compete

Monster Match is an offseason event for FRC teams. Students design, build, and program robots that weigh as much as a person, then team up in alliances of three to score points on a full-size field. This isn't robot combat: nobody is trying to wreck anyone else's robot. Instead, alliances work together to complete the tasks in a game that FIRST designs fresh each season. Matches are fast, loud, and over in a few minutes, so there's always something happening. You don't need to know anything about robotics to enjoy it: pick a team, grab a seat in the stands, and make some noise.

## Trick or treat at the college and career fair

It wouldn't be a Halloween event without candy. Bring a bag and trick or treat your way through the college and career fair, where colleges, employers, and training programs will be on site to talk with students and families about where science, technology, engineering, and skilled trades can lead. Kids collect treats while older students and parents explore their options, whether they're already building robots or just getting curious. Costumes are encouraged for kids and grown-ups alike.

## Plan your visit

- **When:** Saturday, October 31, 2026. Matches run from 8:15 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., so drop in any time and stay as long as you like.
- **Where:** Wayne High School, 9100 Winchester Rd, Fort Wayne, IN 46819

## Why youth robotics matters

The students on the field aren't just building robots. Over a season they design mechanisms, write code, wire electronics, raise money, and run their teams like small businesses, all with adult mentors beside them. It's hands-on practice in the kind of technical work employers are hiring for, and the results follow students well past high school.

{/* Sources verified 2026-09-30. The Brandeis study is independent but FIRST-funded and
    pools all three FIRST programs, not FRC alone. It uses a comparison group, not random
    assignment, so the copy says "more likely" and never claims FIRST causes the outcome. */}

- **Twice as likely to study engineering or computer science.** FIRST alumni are two times more likely than similar students to major in engineering or computer science by their fourth year of college, according to [Brandeis University's 10-year FIRST Longitudinal Study](https://heller.brandeis.edu/cyc/reports/the-first-longitudinal-study-final-report.pdf).
- **STEM majors and STEM careers.** Of FIRST alumni who had declared a college major, 83% chose a STEM field, compared with 61% of similar students. Ten years in, 63% of FIRST alumni work in a STEM field, compared with 42%. ([FIRST impact data](https://www.firstinspires.org/about/impact), from the Brandeis study)
- **Opening doors for young women.** 51% of female FIRST alumni declared a major in engineering or computer science, compared with 16% of similar students. ([FIRST impact data](https://www.firstinspires.org/about/impact))
- **Jobs waiting to be filled.** U.S. manufacturers could need as many as 3.8 million new employees between 2024 and 2033, and 1.9 million of those jobs could go unfilled without more skilled applicants, according to [The Manufacturing Institute and Deloitte](https://themanufacturinginstitute.org/manufacturers-need-as-many-as-3-8-million-new-employees-by-2033/). STEM jobs are projected to grow more than twice as fast as all other jobs over the next decade, according to the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/stem-employment.htm).

That's why The REFINERY exists: so that every student in the region who wants a shot at this has a team, a shop, and the support to build something. Coming out to cheer at Monster Match is one of the easiest ways to show them their community is behind them.
