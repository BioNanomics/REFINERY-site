There's a kid in your hallway or at your kitchen table who would give up every Saturday this winter to build a robot. We've met a few hundred of them.

Finding those kids was never the hard part. The hard part is the price tag, the parts nobody has, the space nobody has, and the adult who has to say yes without knowing a spring from a sprocket. That's the whole reason The REFINERY exists.

We're a program of BioNanomics Inc., a 501(c)(3) here in Northeast Indiana, and we currently back 15 FIRST robotics teams across 12 counties: public schools, homeschool groups, and community teams. The whole model is built so that nobody needs a machine shop, a purchasing line, or an engineer on staff to put a real team on the field. Come eat lunch with us and hear how it actually works.

## Who you'll hear from

- **Doug Horner, founder of BioNanomics:** why he started this, and what he thinks is at stake for our corner of Indiana.
- **The REFINERY crew:** what we do day to day. That means hands-on training in CAD, fabrication, wiring, programming, and project management for students and mentors alike; a stocked parts inventory teams pull from instead of buying; pricing we've negotiated on their behalf; shop space and equipment; help finding mentors; and a hand on the shoulder for whoever ends up running the team.
- **FIRST Indiana Robotics, the statewide FIRST provider:** what's available at every age, from elementary and middle school on up through FIRST Tech Challenge and FIRST Robotics Competition, and what each one really asks of kids, coaches, and space.
- **The Don Wood Foundation:** the grant money out there for a group starting a team, and how to go get it.
- **Fort Wayne Community Schools:** how they staffed it, what it asked of the teacher who volunteered, and what they've watched happen with their students since.
- **Students,** who will tell you more in five minutes than the rest of us will all afternoon.

## What you'll walk out with

A straight answer on what a first year costs, who runs it, how it lines up with CTE and work-based learning if you're a school, and the name of the person to call with questions.

## Come if you're

- An administrator, counselor, CTE or STEM director, or work-based learning coordinator
- A teacher who's been quietly thinking about this
- A homeschool parent or co-op leader
- Someone at a library, 4-H, church, or youth program
- An employer who wants to put your engineers in a room with teenagers

You don't need to know anything about robots. Bring your questions and your doubts about the budget.
