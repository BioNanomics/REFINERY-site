This summer, The REFINERY is running a build program centered on cloning Team 2910's
**Re-Blitz**: CAD, manufacturing, wiring, and programming, start to finish. We're documenting
the whole process openly on Chief Delphi as part of FIRST's Open Alliance, and as part of what
we share back with the broader FIRST community.

## Why we're building it

Most of the teams that use our facility are still early in their FRC journey: 12 of our 14
teams have less than five years of experience, and half are sitting at just two seasons. Cloning
a proven, complex robot like Re-Blitz gives our students and mentors a hands-on blueprint for
building more robust robots and adopting more advanced manufacturing techniques, together. The
finished robot is registered to compete at Boiler Bot Battle in mid-October.

Huge thanks to Team 2910 for being generous with their time and knowledge throughout this,
especially [Nuttyman54](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/u/nuttyman54/summary), we've leaned on their openness more than once.

## Where things stand

The swerve modules are mechanically assembled on all four corners, and the drive base is mostly
done, with custom-machined plates and electronics left. The full Bill of Materials is
also finished, which means we now know exactly what we can make in-house versus what needs to
be sourced.

It hasn't been entirely smooth. We lost time to an AltMill spindle issue traced to its EEPROM
settings, broke several rivet guns trying to set gussets on the drive base (still not sure why),
and had to switch from the 7075 aluminum extrusion Team 2910 used to 6061, since 7075 stock
wasn't available, which puts us in a tighter spot on weight.

Two parts are past what we can machine in-house (a hub/sprocket and the flywheel), so we're
outsourcing those. For the flywheel specifically, we're weighing a hobbyist bronze casting
against a properly machined part, and being upfront about it: at Re-Blitz's original gearing,
the flywheel's theoretical free speed is roughly 24,000 RPM, and we're not comfortable putting
an uninspected cast part into a high-speed shooter assembly at that speed without resolving the
safety question first. We're currently weighing a lower gear ratio, a 3D-printed placeholder,
and cheaper machinable alternatives; nothing decided yet.

Outside the build itself, a local shop offered to laser-cut some of our parts, and we hosted
"From Pits to Prom," a CANOPY-themed party for the upcoming FIRST season.

## Open problems

- Rivet gun failures on gusset installation, root cause unknown
- Flywheel material and gearing still undecided
- Weight impact of the 6061 substitution still being worked through
- Waiting on raw stock availability from our metal supplier
