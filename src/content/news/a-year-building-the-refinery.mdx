A year ago, I took a leap of faith for a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity: building a FIRST
community from the ground up at BioNanomics. I had no idea what I was getting into.

At the time, the space we now call The REFINERY was little more than an empty shell: a mostly
vacant building with a FRC field, a handful of tools, and a lot of potential. I started in late
July. One of my first meetings was with Riley from Fort Wayne Community Schools'
[Amp Lab](https://www.the74million.org/article/building-a-mindset-amp-lab-makes-entrepreneurship-work-skills-its-mission/)
about a school-day program that would launch in our building just a few weeks later, one where
students would help run the space. Walking out of that
meeting, I realized I was in over my head.

## Making it real

We spent the fall turning an empty building into somewhere teams would actually want to be.

First, we gave the space a name. We asked our member teams for suggestions, and the one that
stuck was REFINERY: **R**obotics **E**ducation, **F**abrication, & **I**nnovation **N**exus:
**E**ntrepreneurship for **R**ising **Y**outh. We loved it because it captures what we hope the
program does: refine students into their future selves.

Then came the furniture. I spent more weekends than I can count
at estate sales, loading up furniture and tools and hauling them back one trailer at a time.

Next, we needed teams. I emailed teams across the region, asking whether they'd want to be part of
it. We started hosting Mentor Meetups so the adults behind those teams had a regular
excuse to get in a room together for collaboration and, just as often, commiseration. I called in
friends from across the FIRST community to help me learn all the things I didn't know (and, to be
clear, still don't).

Behind the scenes, we built an extensive robot parts inventory, along with the software to keep
track of it, so a team that needed a part on a Tuesday night didn't have to wait a week for
shipping. And before the season ever started, we equipped 9 FRC teams with swerve drive bases,
which put a drivetrain that many of them had never had within reach.

There was a lot of blood, sweat, and tears in there too.

## Then January hit

Kickoff weekend, we hosted two build-in-three-days teams, Ri3D and KBi3D, while our own 12 FRC teams
launched their seasons around them. Week by week, more teams moved into the building. Mentors
worked side by side across team lines, making sure every team got the help it needed, whether
or not it was their own.

Then we hosted a Week 0 scrimmage, and all but one of our teams showed up. That moment felt like
we were coming out the other side. I remember standing there thinking, "We actually did this!"
Only a few teams loaded out afterwards.

I thought that was our peak. Then I walked in after our first competition and found that
virtually every team had loaded back in overnight instead of going home. Robots and tools
were everywhere. We spent the rest of the season bursting at the seams, and I wouldn't have had it
any other way.

## The season, by the numbers

For a young region, our teams had a year to be proud of. Together, we celebrated **7 wins and 11
awards**, including a Leadership Award Semi-Finalist, 2 Rising All-Stars, and Inspire Award 3rd Place.
Six teams advanced beyond qualifying events, and our average team climbed 6 spots in the district
rankings.

A few moments I keep coming back to:

- [CyBears (10434)](../../teams/frc10434/), in only their second season, won the Mishawaka
  district event alongside [T.H.R.U.S.T. (1501)](../../teams/frc1501/) and jumped from 58th to 28th
  in the district rankings.
- [Olympus Robotics (4982)](../../teams/frc4982/) won the Columbus district event and climbed
  from 71st to 21st.
- [Apex Archers (10172)](../../teams/frc10172/) and [Iron Legends (9119)](../../teams/frc9119/)
  each earned a Rising All-Star Award.
- Maitlen B. from [Robo-Knights (8431)](../../teams/frc8431/) was named a Leadership Award
  Semi-Finalist.
- On the FTC side, [random() (22331)](../../teams/ftc22331/) won their division at the Michiana
  Premier Event and took Inspire Award 3rd Place.

Those winner banners now hang in our windows, and I still smile every time I walk past them.

## "We," not "I"

When the season ended, we finally had time to pause and reflect on what had happened.

You may have noticed that I've said "we" instead of "I" all the way through this, even though I'm
the only full-time REFINERY employee. That's on purpose. None of this was built by one person. The
REFINERY exists because of the mentors, volunteers, students, and supporters who believe in the
vision and keep showing up to do the work.

It hasn't always been smooth. We've made mistakes, taken wrong turns, and racked up plenty of
stories we'll be laughing about for years (a few of them are in the photos below). But we've kept
moving forward together as one big, semi-dysfunctional family.

If you want to know what that family looks like, here's an example: one of
our mentors promised his team that if they qualified for the state championship, he'd shave off
all his hair. They qualified. So he showed up at The REFINERY, and students and mentors from every
team took turns with the clippers until there was nothing left. It wasn't even his team's
celebration anymore; it was everyone's.

This year pushed me to grow in ways I never thought possible. I've spent more time outside my
comfort zone than ever before, and I've learned more than I could have imagined. I'm still in over
my head, but we're only getting started.

If you'd ever like to talk about The REFINERY or FIRST, my door is always open.
[Come visit](../../get-involved/). In the meantime, enjoy these candids of our shenanigans.
